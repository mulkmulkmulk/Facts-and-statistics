Model

Who wins, and who the best players choose

Tekken rates every character separately and matches equal ratings, so win rates get squeezed toward 50%. Character strength also shows in who ends up at the top. This model combines three signals from every ranked match on ewgf.gg.

  1. Top win rate: win rate at the top ranks, pulled toward average by 500 pseudo-games so small samples can't swing it. Direct performance, but compressed by matchmaking.
  2. Top pick rate: share of top-rank games. What the best players choose when they play to win. On its own this is also plain popularity: everyone plays Jin and Kazuya.
  3. Climb lift: the character's share at the top divided by its share at mid ranks (Garyu – Bushin). A character that carries players upward is over-represented at the top, whatever its general popularity. Mid ranks, not all ranks, so beginner-friendly picks aren't penalized for having many new players.

Combining: each signal becomes a z-score ↗ and they're averaged with weights 1 : 1 : 0.75 (win : pick : lift). Lift is weighted slightly lower because that setting reproduced best between independent regions. Weights weren't tuned toward any expected ranking. Each signal covers another's blind spot: lift cancels popularity in pick rate, and win rate checks that popular picks actually perform.

Trust: the same model is re-run on independent slices (Asia, Americas, Europe, other regions, previous patch). "Regions agree" is the rank correlation ↗ between Americas and the rest of the world (1 = same order). A character's rank range is how far it moves between slices.

Matchup consensus

Three views of strength from the head-to-head matchup chart, taken from the highest ranks that have enough data, then combined. Tiers split where the scores jump.

  1. Practical strength: win rate against the opponents people actually play. Answers "what wins in today's meta". Includes a rarity edge: people play worse against characters they rarely see.
  2. Intrinsic strength: the average of all head-to-head win rates, every opponent counted equally. Removes the meta and tells how good the character's matchups are in general. Round-robin ↗
  3. Competitive strength: the matchup chart treated as a zero-sum game (payoff = win rate − 50%). Its Nash equilibrium ↗ is the pick mix nobody can counter. Score = win rate against that mix.

Cleaning: only high ranks. Every matchup is pulled toward 50% by k pseudo-games (Bayesian shrinkage ↗). Each player's side of a match is pooled with the opponent's so rank filtering doesn't favour anyone.

Combining: z-scores ↗ (or Borda ranks ↗), weighted. Uncertainty: a bootstrap ↗ redraws every matchup record 60 times.

Limitation: because matchmaking pairs equal ratings, head-to-head win rates stay close to 50% and understate how far apart characters are.
